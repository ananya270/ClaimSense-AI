## 1. Executive Summary

This report analyzes the false-positive failure modes observed across all evaluated vehicle damage models. In insurance claim verification, excessive false positives lead to adjuster alert fatigue, erroneous repair estimates, and administrative overhead. 

The empirical evaluation of Phase 9.1 confirms that while the 5-epoch Production Baseline (`models/phase5/best.pt`) substantially reduces false alarms compared to the 1-epoch reference model (`90d489e...`), false alarms on daylight clear-coat specular reflections, body panel character lines, and complex wheel geometries remain significant.

## 4. Top False-Positive Case Studies

From the empirical evaluation, three specific test images exhibited the highest false-alarm counts:

### Case 1: `000152.jpg` (9 False Positives)
* **Image Context:** High-contrast daylight photograph showing front three-quarter view of a vehicle with metallic paint.
* **Failure Mechanism:** Sunlight glinting across the hood and front fender character line created high-frequency specular transitions. The model proposed **6 false dent boxes** along the curvature of the fender and **3 false scratch boxes** along the hood edge.
* **Ground Truth:** Single genuine dent on the lower bumper.

## 5. False-Positive Mitigation Recommendations for Phase 9.2

1. **Hard-Negative Daylight Mining:** Integrate the 45 clean walkaround images from `CarDD_DomainAdaptation_v1` into optimization training to penalize glare activations.
2. **Confidence Threshold Elevation for Dents:** Elevate the operational threshold for the `dent` class from $0.25$ to $0.35$ while maintaining lower thresholds for rare classes.
3. **Specular Glare Augmentation:** Simulate synthetic sunlight flares and reflections during training to render detection heads invariant to illumination glints.
4. **Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) Tightening:** Reduce NMS IoU threshold from 0.45 to 0.35 on dent predictions to collapse duplicate proposals along body panels.
